About Me

Naturally curious, I specialise in data analysis and strategic communication for decision-makers. Graduating from ENSAI with a focus on statistics, economics and computer science, I started my career at Insee, working on quarterly national accounts (investment and tax). I then joined the Directorate General of the Treasury, where I was responsible for corporate tax analysis and took part in international tax reforms, before moving to the Inspectorate General of Finance as a data scientist, working on fraud detection and public policy evaluation using large-scale administrative datasets.

I am now a data scientist at the French Senate, providing quantitative support for evaluation and legislative work on economic, social and budgetary issues. Passionate about cybersecurity and social psychology, I also develop R Shiny applications and open-source tools on various topics.

What I Do

Shiny development

I build interactive web applications in R and Python (Shiny, dashboards) for data visualisation, web scraping and operational tools (for example HR management or monitoring dashboards). The aim is to turn complex datasets into accessible, actionable views for decision-makers and operational teams.

Data science

I analyse large and complex datasets using econometrics, machine learning and statistical modelling. My work covers fraud detection, healthcare data (SNDS), public finance and macroeconomic indicators, with a focus on robust methods, reproducible code and clear communication of results.

Writing

I write briefing notes, technical reports and summaries for senior officials and policymakers. I focus on making complex quantitative work understandable, with clear messages, explicit assumptions and transparent limitations.

Economics

My background in economics includes public finances, taxation and macroeconomic analysis. I work on impact assessments, spending reviews and evaluations of fiscal and social policies, combining quantitative models with institutional and qualitative insight.
